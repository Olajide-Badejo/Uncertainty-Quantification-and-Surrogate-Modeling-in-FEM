
## 1. The campaign

A Latin hypercube design of 400 samples over the three independent inputs of the probabilistic model, evaluated by a parametric Abaqus concrete damaged plasticity model of a reinforced concrete member under displacement control. The elastic modulus is a deterministic Eurocode 2 function of the strength and is never an independent input.

| Input | Declared | Mean | Std. dev. | CoV | Min | Max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean compressive strength [MPa] | lognormal, mean 28, CoV 0.1 | 27.998 | 2.806 | 0.1002 | 19.82 | 37.93 |
| Bottom cover [mm] | normal, mu 27, sigma 3 | 27.003 | 3.034 | 0.1124 | 16.50 | 38.75 |
| Top cover [mm] | normal, mu 223, sigma 5 | 222.993 | 5.015 | 0.0225 | 205.55 | 237.91 |

The declared column is read from `configs/probabilistic_model.yaml`, which is the only place in this project a distribution exists (binding law 2). Each marginal's stated basis, from the same file:

- **Mean compressive strength.** JCSS Probabilistic Model Code Part 3 treats concrete compressive strength as lognormal. The mean and CoV match the campaign that was actually executed: the 400 row LHS design realizes Fcm 27.998 +/- 2.806 MPa over [19.82, 37.93].
- **Bottom cover.** Execution tolerance on the bottom cover, which is formed against the soffit and is the better controlled of the two. The realized design gives 27.00 +/- 3.03 mm.
- **Top cover.** Top cover is set from the free surface and is harder to control, so it carries the larger sigma. The realized design gives 222.99 +/- 5.02 mm. This is the variable the campaign censoring is enriched in: failures cluster in its lowest quartile.

## 2. The extraction

Every one of the design rows is reclassified from the ingest artifacts on every run, by the criteria in the `audit` block of `configs/pipeline.yaml`, never from a stored list of sample identifiers. The predecessor project hard coded its surviving sample list as an unexplained literal; that is the defect this stage exists to make impossible.

| Status | Count | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| valid | 198 | present in the extracted data, covering the full displacement range, zero non finite values, monotone displacement, full step time |
| missing | 202 | absent from the extracted data entirely |
| partial | 0 | present but failing at least one completeness criterion |

The classification agrees with the committed 2026-08-28 reference at `data/audit_reference/sample_validity.csv` on all 400 rows, with zero disagreements. Both are derived from the same raw inputs, so exact agreement is the gate rather than a tolerance.

The overall failure rate is 50.5 percent. The failure mode is binary: an analysis either finished completely or left nothing behind. The production solver logs are not preserved in the inherited tree, so the root cause of the losses cannot be diagnosed retrospectively.

## 3. Censoring bias

The failures are not distributed at random over the design. Each input is split into 4 equal count quantile bins of the executed design, so every bin carries the same exposure and a difference in rate is a difference in outcome.

| Input | Q1 (low) | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 (high) | chi squared p | point biserial r |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean compressive strength | 38 % | 51 % | 50 % | 63 % | 0.006 | 0.153 |
| Bottom cover | 48 % | 44 % | 54 % | 56 % | 0.303 | 0.069 |
| Top cover | 76 % | 55 % | 26 % | 45 % | 2.9e-11 | -0.243 |

| Input | Mean when failed | Mean when valid | Welch p | Mann Whitney p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean compressive strength | 28.42 MPa | 27.56 MPa | 0.002 | 5.4e-4 |
| Bottom cover | 27.21 mm | 26.79 mm | 0.166 | 0.136 |
| Top cover | 221.79 mm | 224.22 mm | 8.3e-7 | 9.1e-8 |

Significant at the 5 percent level: mean compressive strength, top cover. Not significant: bottom cover.

The consequence is stated rather than implied: the surviving runs are a biased subsample of the intended design, so every population statistic estimated on them inherits that bias, and every downstream product must either model the censoring or carry the validity domain of section 5.

## 4. The completion probability model

A model of P(complete | mean compressive strength, bottom cover, top cover) fitted over all 400 design rows, 198 complete and 202 not. The estimator that shipped is the gaussian process classifier, the primary of build spec 9.4.

Evaluated by stratified 5 fold cross validation with the feature standardization refitted inside each fold, so no test row contributes to the scaling of its own training set.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| ROC AUC (cross validated) | 0.7018 [0.6576, 0.7419] at 90 percent, 1000 bootstrap resamples |
| Brier score (cross validated) | 0.2193 |
| Brier score of the base rate predictor | 0.2500 |
| Expected calibration error | 0.0257 |

The AUC is modest by construction. Roughly half the design failed, the effect is carried mostly by one input, and no amount of modeling recovers information the solver logs did not preserve. What matters for the use this model is put to is not its ranking power but its calibration: the validity domain thresholds a probability, so that probability has to mean what it says.

## 5. The validity domain

A point is inside the validity domain when the completion model predicts P(complete) >= 0.5 and the point lies inside the box of the executed design, which is the design density condition of build spec 9.4.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Completion threshold | 0.50 |
| Mean compressive strength bounds [MPa] | 19.82 to 37.93 |
| Bottom cover bounds [mm] | 16.50 to 38.75 |
| Top cover bounds [mm] | 205.55 to 237.91 |
| Design points inside | 209 of 400 (52.2 percent) |
| Completed runs inside | 71.2 percent |
| Failed runs inside | 33.7 percent |

The gap between the last two rows is the point. The domain admits 71.2 percent of the runs that completed and only 33.7 percent of those that did not, which is what it means for a domain to track the censoring rather than merely to exist.

`ufem.validity.in_validity_domain(X)` is the only interface to this. It loads the fitted model from the artifact store, rechecks its digest against the one the domain was stamped with, and raises a named diagnostic if the audit stage has not run. It never falls back to an open domain.

## 6. Importance weighting sensitivity

Each of the 198 surviving runs is reweighted by the inverse of its completion probability, so a survivor from a corner where most runs failed stands in for the designed samples that did not survive. If the headline statistics move materially under that reweighting, the censoring is biasing them.

| Quantity | Unweighted mean | Weighted mean | Relative shift | Unweighted CoV | Weighted CoV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peak load [kN] | 38.13 | 38.16 | 0.08 % | 0.0915 | 0.0929 |
| Displacement at peak [mm] | 11.08 | 11.23 | 1.44 % | 0.1754 | 0.1682 |
| Initial stiffness [kN/mm] | 14.14 | 14.02 | -0.88 % | 0.1082 | 0.1081 |
| Absorbed energy to 20 mm [J] | 509.65 | 522.33 | 2.49 % | 0.1727 | 0.1664 |
| Residual load at 20 mm [kN] | 24.73 | 25.89 | 4.68 % | 0.2817 | 0.2790 |
| Softening ratio [-] | 0.6482 | 0.6782 | 4.63 % | 0.2579 | 0.2573 |
| Displacement at half damage saturation [mm] | 8.39 | 8.38 | -0.12 % | 0.1014 | 0.1016 |
| Damage at 10 mm [-] | 0.8858 | 0.8850 | -0.08 % | 0.1577 | 0.1588 |

The largest relative shift is 4.68 percent, on the residual load at 20 mm. The weights themselves span 0.67 to 3.20 after normalization, for an effective sample size of 169.8 against a nominal 198, so the reweighting is not leaning on a handful of survivors.

The shifts are modest, which is itself the finding, and it is the finding that licenses the unweighted statistics for the descriptive purpose they are used for. It does not license extrapolation into the censored corner, which is what the validity domain is for.
