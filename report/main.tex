\begin{abstract}
This report presents the audited results of a \NDesign{} sample Latin hypercube simulation campaign on a reinforced concrete beam modeled with concrete damaged plasticity in Abaqus. Of the \NDesign{} designed samples, \NValid{} completed fully and \NMissing{} produced no output; the failure pattern is shown to be strongly biased in the input space, so the surviving runs are a censored, non random subsample. The censoring is not merely documented but modeled: a completion probability model over the three inputs achieves a cross validated ROC AUC of \CompletionAUC{} with a \CompletionIntervalLevel\,\% bootstrap interval of [\CompletionAUCLow{}, \CompletionAUCHigh{}] and, more importantly for its purpose, is well calibrated (Brier \CompletionBrier{} against \CompletionBrierBaseline{} for the base rate predictor, expected calibration error \CompletionECE{}). That model defines a machine checked validity domain which every downstream product must consult, and an inverse probability weighting study bounds the bias the censoring induces in the headline statistics at \WeightLargestShift\,\% of the mean. Two further findings materially constrain any surrogate: the elastic modulus in the design is an exact deterministic function of the concrete strength, so the campaign has three independent inputs rather than four; and the terminal compressive damage saturates at the material table cap in every run, so it carries no information as a quantity of interest. On the surviving runs the report then builds and measures the full pipeline: elastic registration and functional principal components, one Gaussian process per score and per quantity of interest, and a calibration stage whose simultaneous \MCBandLevel{} percent functional bands and scalar jackknife+ intervals both reach empirical coverage of \BandForceCoverage{} out of sample. The surrogate beats all four mandatory baselines on the four headline scalar quantities and loses to three of them on the whole reconstructed curve, and both halves of that are reported. Sensitivity analysis returns no index at all: every one of the \SensTargets{} sparse chaos expansions failed its trust gate, and the model free roughness measurement that explains why is published in place of the indices. Reliability is reported with Monte Carlo standard errors, surrogate aware bounds, and the \OutOfDomainPct\,\% of the sample mass that falls outside the validity domain. Four ablations measure the design choices against predictions committed before each was run. Every number in this document is regenerated by the pipeline from the raw extracted data and reaches this page through a generated file rather than through a keyboard.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The UFEM project quantifies the effect of material and geometric uncertainty on the nonlinear response of a reinforced concrete member. A parametric Abaqus model was evaluated over a \NDesign{} point Latin hypercube design; the load displacement curve and a global damage evolution curve were extracted from each converged analysis. This document reports what the campaign actually produced and what can honestly be built on it, quantitatively and without retouching: the completion statistics and their bias, a fitted model of that bias, the response statistics over the valid runs, the input to output relations, the registered functional surrogate and its out of sample comparison against mandatory baselines, the conformal calibration of its uncertainty, the sensitivity analysis and the gate that withheld its indices, the propagated failure probabilities with their bounds, the ablations that measure the design choices, and the limitations and outlook those results imply. It replaces all previously circulated summary numbers for this campaign, which descended from a post processing chain with documented defects, including artificially injected variance in the uncertainty stage.

Every figure in this report is written by \texttt{report/figures\_src/make\_figures.py} from the pipeline's artifact store, and every number is written by \texttt{scripts/make\_data\_card.py} into a file this document inputs. The configuration that produced them hashes to \texttt{\ConfigHash}. A test regenerates both and fails if the committed copies have drifted.

\section{The finite element model}
\label{sec:model}
The member is a two dimensional plane stress idealization: a $2000 \times 250$~mm concrete domain of thickness 150~mm, meshed with 637 four node reduced integration elements, with two embedded truss reinforcement layers (three 10~mm bars near the bottom face, three 12~mm bars near the top face, perfect bond). The member is supported by a pin at $x = 200$~mm on the top edge and a roller at $x = 1000$~mm on the bottom edge, and is loaded by a prescribed vertical displacement of 20~mm at the tip of the 800~mm overhang, i.e.\ displacement control throughout, which is why every converged run spans the full displacement range. Concrete is modeled with the damaged plasticity formulation \cite{abaqus2025} (dilation angle $30^\circ$, eccentricity 0.1, $f_{b0}/f_{c0} = 1.16$, $K = 0.667$, viscosity parameter $8 \times 10^{-4}$); the hardening, stiffening, and damage tables are scaled per sample from the sampled strength. Steel is elastic plastic (500~MPa yield, 520~MPa at 1.5\,\% plastic strain).

Four modeling defects are inherited by every result in this report, and they are named here rather than in a footnote because two of them are measured later in this document from two independent directions. The tension softening definition is not regularized by fracture energy while the strength varies between samples, which is a physically incoherent coupling: the softening table's shape is frozen while the material it describes changes, so the energy dissipated per unit crack area is whatever the mesh and the table happen to imply rather than a property of the concrete. Crack band theory \cite{bazant1983} is the reason that matters, and the fracture energy the fib Model Code makes a function of the concrete strength \cite{fib2010} is the quantity the softening branch should have been declared against. No mesh convergence study was performed for the production mesh, despite the project's own written protocol requiring one, so there is no evidence that the localization the model produces is a property of the structure rather than of the discretization. The two embedded reinforcement layers are declared with solid sections on truss elements, which is a modeling error in the input deck rather than a simplification: a truss carries area, and a solid section on it is a declaration the solver reads in a way nobody checked. And the only damage quantity extracted from the analyses is the global maximum compressive damage over the whole model, a single scalar per increment that says how damaged the worst point is and nothing about where or over how much material, which is why the damage response of Section~\ref{sec:stats} is a coarse quantity and why its terminal value carries no information at all.

The first two of those are the reason Section~\ref{sec:sensdiagnosis} finds the response too rough for any smooth metamodel to certify against and Section~\ref{sec:analytic} finds a strength elasticity an order of magnitude above what the reinforcement mechanism implies. Both diagnoses were predicted from this paragraph before either was measured, and the correction of all four is the first item of the outlook in Section~\ref{sec:outlook}.

Their declared marginals and the moments the executed design realized are given in Table~\ref{tab:design}; the lognormal on the concrete strength is the resistance model of the JCSS probabilistic model code \cite{jcss}, and the two cover marginals are execution tolerances. The elastic modulus was generated as the Eurocode~2 secant modulus expression \cite{ec2}, $E = 22000\,(f_{cm}/10)^{0.3}$~MPa. The audit verified that this relation holds exactly in the design data: the rank correlation between $f_{cm}$ and $E$ is one to within floating point (Figure~\ref{fig:collinearity}). The campaign therefore has three independent inputs, not four. Any regression or sensitivity analysis that treats $E$ as a fourth free input is ill posed: variance based indices split the shared effect arbitrarily between two copies of the same random variable. All analyses in this report, and the surrogate specified for the next phase, use the feature vector $(f_{cm}, c_{\mathrm{bot}}, c_{\mathrm{top}})$ only, and the pipeline enforces that in code rather than by convention.

\section{Campaign outcome and censoring}
\label{sec:censoring}
Of \NDesign{} designed samples, \NValid{} completed and appear in the extracted data; \NMissing{} are absent entirely, an overall failure rate of \OverallFailureRate\,\%. There is no partial tier: the pipeline classifies all \NDesign{} rows against five completeness criteria on every run and finds \NPartial{} samples that are present but incomplete. Every present run reached the final pseudo time, spanned the full displacement range, carried no missing values, and descended on the softening branch. The failure mode of this campaign is binary: an analysis either finished or left nothing.

That classification is derived from the extracted data on every run, never from a stored list of sample identifiers. The predecessor pipeline hard coded its \NValid{} sample list as an unexplained literal, which meant the count could not be checked and a change in the data would have gone unnoticed. The reclassification agrees with the independently produced reference classification on all \NDesign{} rows, with zero disagreements.

The failures cluster in the input space (Figure~\ref{fig:censoring}). \subsection{A model of the censoring}
\label{sec:completion}
Documenting the bias is not enough, because every downstream product needs to know, pointwise, whether it is being asked to predict somewhere the solver could actually reach. The pipeline therefore fits a completion probability model $P(\text{complete} \mid f_{cm}, c_{\mathrm{bot}}, c_{\mathrm{top}})$ over all \NDesign{} design rows. The estimator is a \CompletionModel{} classifier with a Mat\'ern kernel on standardized features, fitted with scikit-learn \cite{sklearn}; a regularized logistic regression is available as a pre authorized fallback, and the pipeline records which one shipped and why. Figure~\ref{fig:surface} shows the fitted surface in two dimensional slices, each taken with the third input at its median over the design.

\centering

The white contour is the $P = \DomainThreshold{}$ boundary that defines the validity domain of Section~\ref{sec:domain}. The censored corner is visible in the left panel as the dark region at low top cover and high strength; the bottom cover, centre panel, moves the surface hardly at all.}

The model was evaluated by stratified \CompletionFolds{} fold cross validation, with the feature standardization refitted inside each fold so that no test row contributes to the scaling of its own training set. It achieves a ROC AUC of \CompletionAUC{}, with a \CompletionIntervalLevel\,\% percentile bootstrap interval over \CompletionBootstrap{} resamples of [\CompletionAUCLow{}, \CompletionAUCHigh{}].

That AUC is modest, and it should be read as such. Roughly half the design failed, the effect is carried mostly by one input, and no model recovers information that the production solver logs, which were not preserved, would have contained. But ranking power is not what this model is used for. It is used to threshold a probability, so what matters is whether the probability means what it says. On that criterion the model performs well: the Brier score is \CompletionBrier{} against \CompletionBrierBaseline{} for a predictor that always returns the base rate, and the expected calibration error is \CompletionECE{}. Table~\ref{tab:calibration} and Figure~\ref{fig:calibration} give the reliability diagram; the predicted and empirical rates track each other across every populated bin.

\subsection{The validity domain}
\label{sec:domain}
The completion model defines a validity domain: the region where the model predicts a completion probability of at least \DomainThreshold{} and the query also lies inside the box of the executed design. The second condition is not redundant. A classifier asked about a point far outside the design will still answer, and its answer will be an extrapolation of a smooth kernel rather than evidence. A high completion probability with no design points beneath it is exactly the manufactured confidence this project exists to eliminate.

The domain admits \DomainDesignInside\,\% of the design overall, but it separates the two outcomes as it should: \DomainValidInside\,\% of the runs that completed fall inside it against only \DomainFailedInside\,\% of those that did not. It is stamped into the artifact store together with the fitted model and that model's hash, and it is reachable only through one function, which raises rather than assuming an open domain if the audit stage has not run. The surrogate, the propagation stage, and the interactive interface are each required to consult it.

\subsection{How much does the censoring move the answers?}
\label{sec:weighting}
The validity domain says where predictions are admissible. A separate question is how badly the censoring biases the population statistics estimated on the survivors. To bound that, each of the \WeightNSamples{} surviving runs is reweighted by the inverse of its completion probability, so a survivor from a corner where most runs failed stands in for the designed samples that did not survive, and the headline statistics are recomputed. Table~\ref{tab:weighting} gives both.

The shifts are modest. The peak load mean moves by \WeightPeakShift\,\%, and the largest shift anywhere in the table is \WeightLargestShift\,\% on the \WeightLargestQoI{}, which is the quantity most sensitive to the softening branch and therefore to exactly the configurations that failed. The normalized weights reach at most \WeightMax{}, for an effective sample size of \WeightESS{} against a nominal \WeightNSamples{}, so the reweighting is not being carried by a handful of survivors.

This is a real result and it cuts both ways. It licenses the unweighted descriptive statistics of Section~\ref{sec:stats} for the purpose they are used for, since a few percent of bias in a mean is small next to the spread being reported. It does not license extrapolation into the censored corner, where the issue is not bias in an average but the absence of data altogether. That is what the validity domain is for, and the distinction between the two is the reason both exist.

\subsection{What cannot be recovered}
The production solver logs are not preserved in the surviving tree, so the root cause of the \NMissing{} losses cannot be diagnosed retrospectively. The pattern is consistent with convergence failure in configurations that localize damage early, which low top cover and high strength both promote, but that is a hypothesis the surviving files cannot test. Re running the missing samples under the corrected model configuration is the stated first priority of the follow up campaign, and it is the only thing that would replace the model of this section with a measurement.

\section{Input to output relations}
Figure~\ref{fig:peakvsinputs} shows the peak load against each independent input. Concrete strength dominates, the top cover contributes a secondary positive effect, and the bottom cover a weak negative one. The initial stiffness tells a different story (Figure~\ref{fig:stiffness}): it is governed primarily by the top cover, with strength contributing only weakly through the modulus. Stronger concrete both raises the peak and delays damage accumulation.

These monotone, smooth, moderately interacting relations over three inputs are exactly the regime in which Gaussian process surrogates on a reduced curve representation are expected to perform well \cite{marrel2024}, and in which a linear baseline already explains a substantial fraction of the peak load variance. Sample size is the other half of that argument: \NValid{} surviving runs over three inputs sit comfortably above the runs per input that the computer experiment literature treats as a working minimum for a kernel metamodel \cite{loeppky2009}, which is what makes a Gaussian process the reasonable default here rather than an aspiration. The build specification therefore requires any surrogate to demonstrate out of sample superiority over that baseline rather than to assume it.

\section{Consequences for surrogate construction}
The audit fixes five requirements for the surrogate phase. First, the common displacement grid is legitimate: because loading is displacement controlled, every valid curve spans exactly 0 to 20~mm, and interpolation onto a shared \GridPoints{} point grid loses nothing at the resolution the curves carry. Second, duplicated solver cutback time stamps occur in a known subset of the jobs and must be removed by a strict monotonicity filter before interpolation. Third, the feature vector is three dimensional; the modulus is derived, never regressed on. Fourth, the displacement at peak varies by more than a factor of two across the family, so the curves exhibit phase variability as well as amplitude variability, and a registration step before dimensionality reduction is required to avoid the known artifacts of principal component analysis on misaligned peaked curves. Fifth, and specific to this phase, every product downstream of the surrogate must consult the validity domain of Section~\ref{sec:domain}, and any population level claim must either carry the weighting sensitivity of Section~\ref{sec:weighting} or state that it does not.

\section{Registration and reduction}
\label{sec:registration}
The audit established that these curves vary in phase as well as in amplitude: the displacement at peak ranges over more than a factor of two across the family. That fact governs how the curves may be reduced to a small number of coordinates, which is the step every surrogate in this project is built on.

Principal component analysis is a linear method, and a linear basis cannot represent a shift. Asked to describe a family whose features sit at different abscissae, it approximates the shift by superposing fixed shapes, which costs components that carry no mechanical meaning and, worse, smears the peak of any truncated reconstruction: averaging curves whose maxima are not aligned produces a mean that is flatter and lower than any individual curve, which is the amplitude and phase confounding that functional data analysis exists to separate \cite{marron2015}. For a structural surrogate that is the least acceptable artifact available, because the peak load is the quantity the reliability analysis of a later phase thresholds. The remedy is to separate the two kinds of variation before reducing either, so that amplitude and phase each get a basis suited to it.

This pipeline does that with the square root slope framework \cite{tucker2013,srivastava2016}, through the fdasrsf package \cite{fdasrsf}. Each curve is first reparameterized by arc length in a normalized load displacement plane, using the fixed global normalizers $P_0 = 40$~kN and $u_0 = 20$~mm read from the configuration rather than per curve values; normalizing each curve by its own peak would make every curve reach unity and destroy exactly the amplitude information the surrogate exists to predict. The family is then registered elastically, which yields a set of aligned amplitude functions and a set of warping functions $\gamma$ carrying the phase. The warps are mapped through $\psi = \sqrt{\dot{\gamma}}$ to the tangent space at their Karcher mean, because the warps themselves are monotone functions pinned at both ends and so do not form a vector space: a principal component of raw $\gamma$ would in general not be a warp at all. Every one of the \NValid{} warps produced here is monotone with $\gamma(0) = 0$ and $\gamma(1) = 1$ to within $10^{-8}$, which is the gate this phase is required to pass.

Figure~\ref{fig:registration} shows the family before and after registration and Figure~\ref{fig:warps} the warping functions themselves. Three separate reductions follow, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:reduction}: one on the registered amplitude, one on the phase, and one on the damage curves, which are deliberately left unregistered because they are monotone saturating curves that all terminate at the same material table cap, so they carry no phase variation worth separating. Components are retained to \VarianceTarget\,\% of variance in each block. The registered amplitude needs \ComponentsAmplitude{}, with the first component alone carrying \AmplitudePCOne\,\% of the variance; its leading loadings are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:loadings}. The phase block needs \ComponentsPhase{} and the damage block \ComponentsDamage{}, and both of those numbers are larger than the build specification anticipated. The damage family in particular was expected to be describable in about two components; it reaches \VarianceDamage\,\% only at \ComponentsDamage{}, because a long tail of small modes follows a strong leading pair. These are reported as measured rather than adjusted.

\subsection{Did registration earn its place?}
\label{sec:ablation1}
Because registration is the methodological centre of this pipeline, it is tested rather than asserted, and the test was specified before it was run. The predicted outcome was committed to \texttt{docs/ABLATIONS.md} in a commit that precedes the one adding the measurement script, so the prediction cannot have been fitted to a number already seen. Three model free metrics compare the registered reduction against principal component analysis applied directly to the curves on the displacement grid, with no surrogate involved on either side, because the claim under test is about the representation.

Two of the three predictions held and one failed, and Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports all three. The component count is the clearest result: the unregistered family needs \AblationComponentsUnregistered{} components to reach \VarianceTarget\,\% of its variance where the registered family needs \AblationComponentsRegistered{}, a ratio of \AblationComponentRatio{} against a predicted two to three. The peak load bias also came out as predicted in sign and in ordering: reconstructing at the common rank of \AblationRank{} components under predicts the mean peak load by \AblationPeakBiasUnregistered~N without registration against \AblationPeakBiasRegistered~N with it, or \AblationPeakBiasUnregisteredPct\,\% against \AblationPeakBiasRegisteredPct\,\% of the mean peak. The registered reconstruction is carried back through its own warps before its peak is measured, so neither side is flattered by being scored in a space where the phase never had to be reproduced.

The third prediction was wrong. I expected the second principal component of the unregistered family to be visibly derivative shaped, correlating with $\mathrm{d}\bar{f}/\mathrm{d}u$ above 0.7 and far above the registered value, on the reasoning that a small shift of a function is to first order the negative of its derivative times the shift. The measured correlations are \AblationDerivCorrUnregistered{} unregistered and \AblationDerivCorrRegistered{} registered: both negligible, and running the wrong way. Inspecting all six leading components afterwards, which is a diagnostic and not a prediction, locates the derivative structure in the first component instead (\AblationDerivCorrPCOneUnregistered{} unregistered against \AblationDerivCorrPCOneRegistered{} registered), where the ordering does run as the mechanism argues. The honest reading is that the first component here is not a pure amplitude mode, because in this design the curves that peak late are also, through the concrete strength, the curves that peak high, so amplitude and phase are correlated rather than orthogonal. The mechanism may well be sound; my specification of where to look for it was not, and the number selected after the fact is not promoted to a confirmation. Registration is retained on the strength of the component count and the peak bias, which is two of the three legs it was asked to stand on.

\section{Gaussian process surrogate and fold honest validation}
\label{sec:surrogate}
The reduction of Section~\ref{sec:registration} turns each of the \NValid{} curves into a
handful of scores; this phase learns the map from the three independent inputs to those
scores, and reports whether that map is worth having by measuring it against dumb baselines
in the same harness, never a friendlier one.

\subsection{One Gaussian process per score and per scalar}
One exact Gaussian process is fitted per retained reduction score and per scalar quantity of
interest, GPyTorch \cite{gpytorch} on CPU in float64: a constant mean, a Mat\'ern 5/2 kernel with automatic
relevance determination over the three standardized inputs, and a homoscedastic noise fitted
under a LogNormal hyperprior rather than a hard floor, for the reason ground rule 4 exists:
a fitted floor at the solver's numerical resolution reproduced the interpolate the scatter
failure of the predecessor project on 36 of 45 targets, so the prior is centered instead on
the share of a standardized target's variance the three inputs are not expected to explain,
and the fit then moves off that center by up to a factor of 25. \NSurrogateTargets{} targets
in total: \SurrogateComponentsAmplitude{} registered amplitude scores,
\SurrogateComponentsDamage{} damage scores, plus the eight scalar quantities of interest and
three landmarks, each with its own process. Every fit is a multi start optimization from
\NGPRestarts{} seeded initializations, best marginal likelihood wins, and every restart is
logged rather than only the winner.

Two reduction blocks are capped rather than fitted to the reduce stage's own 99 percent
target. The phase block reaches 99 percent at \PhaseComponentsNeeded{} components, which is
not a ratio 198 samples support for independent processes, so only
\SurrogateComponentsPhase{} are fitted, carrying \PhaseVarianceCarried\,\% of the phase
block's variance; the rest is carried forward as reconstruction residual variance rather than
being predicted. A fourth reduction block, the displacement coordinate along the arc length
stations, is added at this phase: the registration of Section~\ref{sec:registration}
reparameterizes every curve by arc length, so the registered amplitude alone is force against
a parameter, not force against displacement, and recovering the physical curve needs the
displacement half as well. It is monotone, so it is represented the same way the phase warps
are, through the square root slope tangent space, and is capped for the same reason:
\SurrogateComponentsDisplacement{} of the \DisplacementComponentsNeeded{} components a
99 percent target would need, carrying \DisplacementVarianceCarried\,\% of that block's
variance. Both caps are measured costs recorded in the surrogate's manifest, not assumptions.

Scalar quantities of interest are always predicted by their own process and never read off the
reconstructed curve, which build spec 10.4 reserves as a consistency diagnostic; the two
differ by construction, because the curve is a truncated reconstruction while the scalar
process is fitted on the exact measurement. The reconstructed curve's own median relative
$L_2$ error against the training family is \SurrogateForceReconMedian\,\%, and this is the in
sample floor the fold honest numbers below are compared against.

\subsection{The baseline gate}
\label{sec:baselines}
Build spec 10.5 requires the surrogate to beat four baselines that share nothing with it, in
the same cross validation harness, or to report the loss rather than the tuned improvement:
the training mean curve, ordinary least squares on the three standardized inputs, the full
quadratic polynomial chaos expansion, and a three nearest neighbour inverse distance average.
None of the four has a hyperparameter fitted on this data. Scalars are compared by closed form
leave one out over all \NValid{} runs (Dubrule \cite{dubrule1983}; Rasmussen and Williams
\cite{rasmussen2006}, section~5.4.2), which reuses the hyperparameters fitted on all runs and states that
approximation rather than hiding it; curves are compared by \ValidationFolds{} grouped folds,
because a curve level leave one out would refit the registration and every principal
component basis 198 times, an arithmetic cost of about 42 minutes this project chose not to
pay for a cross check the grouped folds already answer honestly (both the registration
reference and every basis are refitted inside each fold, which is the fold honesty build spec
16.3 requires).

Table~\ref{tab:baselines} is the result. The gate of build spec 10.5 is decided on the four
headline quantities of interest declared in configuration, and it has \ValidationGatePassed{}:

\section{Ablations: what the design choices cost}

committed before it is measured. That rule is what separates an ablation from an illustration,
and it is enforced by the commit order rather than by assertion: for every row of

recomputation reproduces the validation stage's committed median curve errors to zero deviation

\subsection{Autoencoder plus Gaussian process}
The predecessor project's second architecture, rebuilt without its defects: group aware folds,
the production kernel with its fitted noise on the latent codes, the three input feature
contract, and everything refitted inside the fold. Learning the representation and the kernel
on the same small sample is the setting in which a deep kernel is known to overfit its marginal
likelihood and lose the calibration a Gaussian process was chosen for \cite{ober2021}, which is
why this path enters the report as an ablation with the encoder fitted separately rather than as
the shipped surrogate. The force autoencoder carries
\AblationTwoForceLatent{} latent coordinates, between the \SurrogateComponentsAmplitude{}
amplitude components the production basis retains and the score quantities it predicts to rebuild
one curve, so the comparison is not a budget handicap; the damage autoencoder carries
\AblationTwoDamageLatent{} against \SurrogateComponentsDamage{}. Its damage decoder is the one
idea salvaged from the old tree, softplus increments through a cumulative sum, which makes a
decoded damage curve non decreasing by construction. The terminal renormalization that followed
it in the original file is deliberately absent, because dividing every decoded curve by its own
last value forces them all to end at the same height and destroys the amplitude a surrogate
exists to predict; a unit test asserts that the spread of terminal values is not zero, so the
defect cannot come back unnoticed.

\AblationTwoClaimsHeld{} of the three committed claims held. The force curve error is
\AblationTwoForceLTwo\,\% against \CurveLTwoGP\,\% for the production pipeline, and the damage
curve error \AblationTwoDamageLTwo\,\% against \AblationTwoProductionDamageLTwo\,\%. The peak load
read off the decoded curve reaches an out of sample $R^2$ of \AblationTwoPeakRSq{}, against
\AblationTwoProductionScalarPeakRSq{} for the production process that predicts the peak directly
and \AblationTwoProductionCurvePeakRSq{} for the production curve read the same way the
autoencoder's is. That last number is the fair comparison and it was not the one predicted: the
asymmetry between a dedicated scalar process and a peak read off a curve was flagged in advance,
and reporting both is the answer to it.

\subsection{Five member deep ensemble}
Five seeded multilayer perceptrons mapping the three standardized inputs straight to the 201 point
curve, each with a Gaussian negative log likelihood head, combined as the mixture whose variance
is the mean member variance plus the variance of the member means. A learned operator is the
obvious modern alternative to a reduced basis and a kernel, and how the two families compare in
data efficiency at small sample size is an open comparison in the literature
\cite{westermann2026}. This project declines to settle it by assertion, which is why the network
family appears here as a measured ablation on this campaign rather than as the shipped model or
as an exclusion asserted in a sentence. \AblationThreeClaimsHeld{} of
the four claims held, and the split is the interesting part. The ensemble beats the production
pipeline on both error metrics, \AblationThreeRMSE~N against \AblationThreeProductionRMSE~N
pointwise and \AblationThreeForceLTwo\,\% against \CurveLTwoGP\,\% per curve, and loses on both
uncertainty metrics, a negative log predictive density of \AblationThreeNLPD{} against
\AblationThreeProductionNLPD{} and a pointwise coverage of \AblationThreeCoverage{} against
\AblationThreeProductionCoverage{} at a nominal \MCBandLevel{} percent. Neither side is calibrated
here, which is the point of the comparison: the production pipeline's raw pointwise variance is
the linear amplitude propagation of Section~\ref{sec:surrogate} and it under covers too, which is
exactly what the conformal step of Section~\ref{sec:calibration} exists to fix and what a deep
ensemble has no equivalent of. An ensemble that predicts better and quantifies worse is the
literature's own description of the method, arrived at here on this campaign.

\subsection{B-spline coefficient regression}
\AblationFourBasisFunctions{} cubic basis functions on the displacement grid with the interior
knots at quantiles of a mixture density centered on each fold's own median displacement at peak,
one production Gaussian process per coefficient, and a pointwise variance propagated through the
fixed basis exactly as the amplitude scores are. \AblationFourClaimsHeld{} of three claims held.
It is the most accurate curve model in this report, \AblationFourForceLTwo\,\% median relative
$L_2$ against \CurveLTwoGP\,\%, which is better than the competitiveness that was predicted and
so counts as a refutation. And it is the worst peak model in this report:
\AblationFourPeakBias~N of peak load bias against \AblationFourProductionPeakBias~N for the
production pipeline, which is the fixed basis rounding the peak of a family whose peak moves,
the same mechanism ablation 1 measured on the unregistered principal component basis and a factor
of ten larger here. The curvature claim came apart in the middle: the predicted peak is indeed
blunter, and by a wide margin, but its curvature error of \AblationFourCurvatureError{} against a
true mean curvature magnitude of \AblationFourTruthCurvature{} is smaller than the production
pipeline's \AblationFourProductionCurvatureError{}, so the second half of that claim failed. The
production reconstruction gets the sharpness wrong in the other direction, which is a finding
about the warped reconstruction rather than about splines.

\subsection{Sobol sequence against a thinned design}
The one ablation that is not about the model. At $n$ in $\{64, 128, 198\}$, subsets of the 198
runs are selected \AblationFiveRepetitions{} times each, either uniformly at random, which stands
in for thinning the Latin hypercube design, or by letting a scrambled Sobol sequence claim the
nearest unused real run in standardized coordinates. The production peak load process is fitted
on each subset and scored by its closed form leave one out error. What this measures is design
sensitivity on an inherited censored campaign, not what a Sobol campaign would have produced, and
that limitation is in the artifact as well as in this sentence.

\AblationFiveClaimsHeld{} of three claims held. The Sobol guided selection wins at $n = 64$,
\AblationFiveSobolSmall~N against \AblationFiveRandomSmall~N of leave one out root mean square
error, and the gap clears the repetition spread of \AblationFiveSpreadSmall~N. It wins by
\AblationFiveAdvantageSmall\,\%, which is more than the under ten percent that was predicted, so
the size claim failed in the direction of the effect being larger than expected. The advantage
falls to \AblationFiveAdvantageMiddle\,\% at $n = 128$ as predicted, and at $n = 198$ both
selections return the whole population, so the two are the same set by construction and the
difference is exactly zero. That last cell is arithmetic rather than convergence, it was written
down as such before the study ran, and the script asserts it rather than reporting it as a
measurement.

\subsection{What the four together say}
Three independent curve models now beat the shipped pipeline at curve level, and one of them,
the B-spline basis, does it while being far worse at the peak. Read with the last column of
Table~\ref{tab:baselines}, where the linear, quadratic chaos and nearest neighbour baselines do
the same, the pattern is consistent and it points at one place: the reconstruction path. A
direct curve model predicts heights on a fixed abscissa, while the production pipeline predicts
amplitude scores, phase scores and a displacement coordinate and then composes them, so an error
in a phase score moves the whole curve sideways and costs $L_2$ in a way a height error cannot.
What the reconstruction buys in exchange is the peak, which it gets right to
\AblationFourProductionPeakBias~N where the best curve level rival is off by
\AblationFourPeakBias~N, and a variance with a defensible shape to calibrate. For a report whose
headline quantity is a peak load and whose deliverable is a calibrated interval, that is the
right trade, but it is a trade rather than a win, and Section~\ref{sec:limitations} says so.

\section{Calibrated uncertainty}
\label{sec:calibration}
A surrogate that predicts well and quantifies badly is worse than useless in a reliability
setting, because the number it produces looks like a measurement. The predecessor project is
the cautionary case: it floored its predicted standard deviation, multiplied every variance by
a constant greater than one, and injected multiplicative noise, and every interval it published
descended from those three operations. This section is built so that the same sentence cannot
be written about this work. Exactly two operations touch a predictive distribution here, and
both are measurements rather than choices.

The first is one out of fold variance scaling factor per target, the square root of the mean
squared standardized leave one out residual. Applying it drives the predictive variance
adequacy, defined as $\mathrm{PVA} = \log\!\big(\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_i z_i^2\big)$ with
$z_i = (y_i - \mu_{-i}(x_i))/\sigma_{-i}(x_i)$, to zero out of fold, which is the recalibration
order build spec 11.3 prescribes. It is reported per target whatever it comes out at. The
second is a conformal quantile applied on top of the recalibrated model. That is where the
coverage guarantee comes from, and it is distribution free: it needs the runs to be
exchangeable and nothing else, in particular nothing about the Gaussian process being right.
The order matters only for the shape of the band, never for its validity, which is precisely
why the correction factor can be reported honestly instead of being tuned.

\subsection{Scalars: jackknife+ with sigma normalized scores}
Each scalar quantity of interest gets nonconformity scores
$R_i = |y_i - \mu_{-i}(x_i)| / \sigma_{-i}(x_i)$ built from the closed form leave one out, which
is the sigma normalized score a conformal wrapper around a Gaussian process is built on
\cite{jaber2025}, and a jackknife+ interval \cite{barber2021} at $\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.05\}$. The interval at a query point is a quantile
over the whole ensemble of leave one out models evaluated \emph{there}, not a residual quantile
around one prediction, and the same block inverse identity that gives Dubrule's leave one out
diagonal gives that ensemble from the one Cholesky already computed.

Measuring the coverage of such an interval at a training point takes one more step, and
skipping it changes the answer. The $n-1$ models that set the interval at $x_j$ were all fitted
on $y_j$, so at $x_j$ they interpolate rather than predict, and the measured coverage comes out
conservative for exactly the quantities whose in sample fit most exceeds their out of sample
fit. On this campaign that read five to seven points of coverage too high on displacement at
peak and initial stiffness. Every coverage in Table~\ref{tab:conformalscalars} is therefore
measured by a nested leave one out: the query point is removed first, and the ensemble, the
scores and the scaling factor are all built inside what is left, so nothing that sets the
interval at a point has seen that point.

reuse caveat that build spec 11.1 asks to be stated costs nothing measurable here. It would not
follow at a smaller $n$, or with a kernel whose hyperparameters moved more between folds.

\subsection{Curves: simultaneous sup norm bands}
\label{sec:functionalbands}
For the curve outputs the band has to hold at every abscissa at once, or a reader is invited to
read a pointwise interval as if it were a statement about the whole response. The score is the
worst standardized pointwise miss over the curve,
$R_i = \sup_u |y_i(u) - \mu_{-i}(u)| / s(u)$, with the modulation $s(u)$ the Gaussian process
posterior predictive standard deviation of the curve, truncation residual included; the
$\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha) \rceil$th order statistic of those scores scales the band, and coverage
is then simultaneous by construction of the supremum. This modulated functional band on finite
element softening curves is the second increment this project claims, and it is claimed
modestly: the construction is that of Diquigiovanni, Fontana and Vantini
\cite{diquigiovanni2021,diquigiovanni2022}, and what is new here
is the modulation coming from a Gaussian process posterior over a registered functional
representation rather than from a residual envelope.

The band exists where the response varies. Both families have abscissae at which all \NValid{}
runs take the same value, so a standardized residual there is $0/0$: the origin for the load
displacement family, and \BandDamageExcluded{} of \GridPoints{} stations for the damage family,
which are the first few tenths of a millimetre before damage initiates and everything past
\BandDamageEnd\,mm, where every run has reached the same saturated value. Those stations carry
no calibration information and are excluded from the supremum, leaving \BandForceStations{} and
\BandDamageStations{} stations respectively. Nothing is floored to keep them, because a floor
under a variance that is genuinely zero is the fabricated uncertainty this whole section exists
to avoid, and the damage saturation showing up as a degenerate domain is a finding about the
data rather than a numerical inconvenience.

\subsection{The calibration gate}
Build spec 11.5 forbids any propagated number until three things hold, and the stage checks all
three itself and exits nonzero if any fails. The simultaneous \CalibrationLevel{}\,\% band must
reach a leave one out empirical coverage whose Wilson interval contains $0.90$: measured
\BandForceCoverage{} on the load displacement curves, Wilson interval
[\BandForceWilsonLow{}, \BandForceWilsonHigh{}], and \BandDamageCoverage{} on the damage curves
with the same interval. The scalar jackknife+ intervals must do the same per headline quantity:
measured \JackPMaxCoverage{} for peak load, \JackUPeakCoverage{} for displacement at peak,
\JackKZeroCoverage{} for initial stiffness and \JackEAbsCoverage{} for absorbed energy, all with
Wilson interval [\JackPMaxWilsonLow{}, \JackPMaxWilsonHigh{}]. Every one of these sits inside
the exact finite sample bracket the conformal construction guarantees, which at \NValid{} runs
is $[0.900, 0.905]$.

The third criterion is the one build spec 11.5 states visually, that the PIT heatmap shows no
gross U shape on the softening branch, and a visual criterion inside a machine checked gate is
not a criterion. It is implemented as a statistic: the fraction of PIT values falling in the
outer two deciles over the post peak part of every curve, which a calibrated predictive
distribution puts at \PitOuterMassNominal{} by definition and which rises above that precisely
when the tails fill in. The threshold is \PitOuterMassMax{}, committed before the first
measurement was taken. Measured, the statistic falls from \PitOuterMassBefore{} before the
variance scaling to \PitOuterMassAfter{} after it, so the uncalibrated model sat within a
percentage point of failing this criterion, and the measured scaling is what moved it. The gate
\CalibrationGatePassed{}.

A closing caveat, because the bands in Figure~\ref{fig:conformalband} are wide and the reason is
not the calibration. A simultaneous band over two hundred abscissae is scaled by a supremum, and
the supremum of a standardized residual field has a long right tail; the multiplier here is
\BandForceScale{}. Part of that width is the price of simultaneity and the rest is the curve
level accuracy reported in Section~\ref{sec:baselines}, where the surrogate does not beat its
baselines. The band is honest about a model that is mediocre at whole curves, which is the
correct behavior and an argument for improving the model rather than the band.

\section{Global sensitivity}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
Because the three inputs are genuinely independent after the reparameterization of
Section~\ref{sec:design}, ordinary Sobol machinery is valid here. Had the elastic modulus
stayed a feature alongside the strength it is an exact function of, every Saltelli estimate in
this section would have been structurally wrong, because the estimator resamples input columns
independently and there is no independent resample of two columns that are the same random
variable. That is not a caveat about precision. It is the difference between a number and a
number shaped object, and it is the whole reason the standard machinery can be used at all.

The primary construction is a sparse polynomial chaos expansion
\cite{blatman2011,luethen2021}, for two reasons that both
matter at \NValid{} points. The first is that its Sobol indices are not estimated. Expanding a
response on a basis that is orthonormal under the input distribution makes the response
variance the sum of the squared non constant coefficients, and the partial variance of any
input set the sum over the terms supported on exactly that set, so the indices are read off the
fitted coefficients with no sampling error whatsoever. The second is that sparsity is what
makes the fit possible: the full degree \SensDegree{} tensor basis over three inputs is 56
terms against \NValid{} points, so an ordinary least squares fit would be a rich model fitted
with nothing left over to check it with. Hyperbolic truncation at $q = \SensHyperbolicQ$ cuts
the candidate set to \SensCandidateTerms{} by penalizing high order interactions more than high
order main effects, which is the right prior for a mechanical response, and least angle
regression then selects a subset of those under a corrected leave one out criterion, in
OpenTURNS \cite{openturns}.

The cross check draws Sobol indices from the Gaussian process posteriors instead, and the point
of it is not a second opinion but an uncertainty. A polynomial chaos index is a single number
with no error bar, and a reader has no way to tell a well determined 0.8 from a 0.8 that would
have been 0.5 on a different sample of the same size. So \SensRealizations{} conditional
realizations are drawn from each fitted posterior, a Saltelli estimate is computed on each one,
and the index is reported as the distribution over them. Realizations rather than the posterior
mean, because the mean of a Gaussian process is smoother than any of its draws and its indices
are biased toward whichever input dominates. Two surrogate families with different failure
modes agreeing on an index is the strongest validation available without new finite element
runs, and where they disagree the disagreement is the finding.

\subsection{The trust gate, and what it decided}
\label{sec:q2gate}
A Sobol index computed from a metamodel is a statement about the metamodel, and it inherits
whatever the metamodel got wrong. Build specification 12.1 therefore ties publication to the
corrected leave one out $Q^2$ of each expansion, on the thresholds of the Blatman and Sudret
line of work \cite{blatman2011}: at or above \SensQTwoValues{} the index values are published; between
\SensQTwoRankings{} and \SensQTwoValues{} only the ordering is, with the values carried as
indicative; below \SensQTwoRankings{} nothing derived from that expansion is published. The
thresholds were written into \texttt{src/ufem/sensitivity.py} before the first expansion was
fitted, and they are in the code rather than in the configuration for the same reason the
calibration thresholds of Section~\ref{sec:calibration} are: a threshold that can be edited
without a code change is a threshold that will be edited when it fails.

It failed. Of the \SensTargets{} expansions fitted, \SensPublishValues{} reached the value
publishing level, \SensPublishRankings{} reached the ranking level, and \SensWithheld{} did not
clear either. The best of them, \SensQTwoBestTarget{}, reaches \SensQTwoMax{} and the worst
\SensQTwoMin{}. No Sobol index value and no input ranking from this campaign is published in
this report. Table~\ref{tab:sensgate} and Figure~\ref{fig:sensgate} are what the phase produces
instead: the measurement that decided it, per target, with the two thresholds drawn on.

That is a result rather than a failure to produce one, and it is worth saying why it is worth
having. The predecessor project ran this same analysis and published first order indices
summing to 0.23 against total indices near 0.88 for three independent inputs, which is the
signature of a noise dominated surface, and it published them because nothing in that pipeline
ever asked whether the surface being decomposed described the beam. This pipeline asks, gets
the answer no, and says so.

\subsection{Why the gate fired}
\label{sec:sensdiagnosis}
Two independent measurements say the same thing, and neither of them is about the expansion.

The first comes from the surrogate of Section~\ref{sec:surrogate}. Its Gaussian processes fit a
nugget alongside the kernel, and in standardized target units the two together account for the
whole variance, so the nugget is what an entirely different model family concluded the three
inputs do not determine. Read as a ceiling on any smooth metamodel's $Q^2$, it is
\SensCeilingPMax{} for peak load, \SensCeilingUPeak{} for displacement at peak,
\SensCeilingKZero{} for initial stiffness and \SensCeilingEAbs{} for absorbed energy, against
measured $Q^2$ values of \SensQTwoPMax{}, \SensQTwoUPeak{}, \SensQTwoKZero{} and
\SensQTwoEAbs{}. Three of the four expansions sit at or near the ceiling their own data allows.
A richer basis would not move them.

The ceiling is only readable where the Gaussian process fit is not itself degenerate, and on
\SensPinned{} of the \SensTargets{} targets it is not: those processes drove every length scale
to its configured lower bound with a nugget near zero, which is the interpolate the scatter
failure of Section~\ref{sec:surrogate} rather than a claim that the response is explainable.
Their ceilings are withheld from Table~\ref{tab:sensgate} rather than printed, and every one of
them is a trailing principal component that carries almost no variance to begin with. The four
headline quantities are not among them.

The second is model free and is the more direct of the two. For every training point, take its
nearest neighbour in the standardized input space, and look at the closest
\SensRoughFraction\,\% of those pairs, which are separated by at most \SensRoughSeparation{} in
standardized units, well inside every fitted correlation length. The median absolute difference
in peak load across those nearly coincident designs is \SensRoughPMax{} times the peak load
standard deviation over the whole campaign; for displacement at peak it is \SensRoughUPeak{}
and for initial stiffness \SensRoughKZero{}. Two beams that differ by a fraction of a
millimetre of cover and a fraction of a megapascal of strength produce responses that differ by
a large share of the entire campaign's spread. The response surface varies on a scale finer
than \NValid{} points can resolve, and no smooth metamodel of any family will certify against
it.

The likely cause is in the finite element model itself and was named in
Section~\ref{sec:model} long before any of this was measured. The inherited campaign varies the
concrete strength while the tension softening table stays shape frozen and unregularized by a
fracture energy, and it ran on one mesh with no convergence study. Under that combination the
localization pattern is free to jump between neighbouring designs, which is exactly a response
that is deterministic in principle and rough in practice. Correcting it is the first item of
the follow up campaign, and this section is the quantitative argument for it: not that the
model is inelegant, but that it makes the campaign's sensitivity structure unmeasurable.

\subsection{The two constructions do not agree, and that is the same finding}
\label{sec:sensagreement}
Build specification 12.2 makes agreement between the two routes the acceptance criterion and
requires a disagreement to be investigated in writing rather than averaged away. The criterion
is containment: the analytic chaos index has no interval of its own, because it is a
decomposition of a fitted expansion rather than an estimate of anything, so the test is whether
it falls inside the Gaussian process posterior 90 percent interval. It does so on
\SensAgreeCount{} of \SensAgreeRows{} index rows. The two constructions disagree on most of
the response.

That result would be alarming if the gate of Section~\ref{sec:q2gate} had passed. It is not
alarming here, and the investigation is worth setting out in full, because a reader is entitled
to ask whether the withheld verdict is a property of the campaign or of my implementation.

The first thing checked was the one approximation this stage introduces. Drawing 200 joint
realizations at \SensDesignRows{} points is not a factorization anybody can afford, so the
realizations come from a pathwise construction whose prior term is a random Fourier feature
expansion, with the conditioning update kept exact. Raising the feature count from 1024 to
16384, which more than halves the measured kernel deviation, moves the posterior median first
order index for peak load by 0.004 and for initial stiffness by 0.023, against gaps to the
chaos value of 0.11 and 0.31. The approximation is not what separates the two routes.

The second is structural, and it accounts for most of the gap. Least angle regression selects a
sparse basis, and on \SensNoInteractionExpansions{} of the \SensTargets{} expansions it selected
no interaction term at all, so the chaos total index equals the chaos first order index exactly
on \SensAdditiveSlots{} of the \SensInputSlots{} input slots and the median chaos interaction
share is \SensChaosInteraction{}. A Gaussian process realization is a generic rough function
whose interaction is never exactly zero: the posterior does that on
\SensPosteriorAdditiveSlots{} slots and its median interaction share is
\SensPosteriorInteraction{}. Two model families that disagree about whether the response is
additive will disagree about total indices whatever the data says, and the counts show exactly
that shape: the first order indices agree on \SensAgreeFirst{} of \SensAgreeFirstRows{} rows and
the total indices on only \SensAgreeTotal{} of \SensAgreeTotalRows{}.

The third is that a share of the misses are boundary technicalities rather than disagreements
about the beam. Where least angle regression drops an input entirely the chaos index is exactly
zero, while the posterior interval for a negligible input starts just above zero, so the two
differ by a thousandth and the containment test records a miss. Allowing a tolerance of 0.01
raises the count to \SensAgreeWithinOnePct{} of \SensAgreeRows{}, and 0.05 raises it to
\SensAgreeWithinFivePct{}. The median gap among the misses is \SensGapMedian{} against a median
posterior interval width of \SensPosteriorWidthMedian{}.

What is left after those three is a genuine disagreement about how the explained part of the
variance is attributed, and it is exactly what two different wrong models should be expected to
produce. Neither construction reaches $Q^2 = \SensQTwoRankings{}$ on any target, so neither is
describing the response well enough for its variance decomposition to be the response's. There
is no reason two such models should coincide, and the measurement is that they do not. The
disagreement and the gate are one finding stated twice, and the correct conclusion from both is
the one Section~\ref{sec:sensdiagnosis} reaches: this campaign's sensitivity structure is not
measurable from these 198 runs.

One presentational point, because it is the sort of thing that quietly inflates confidence
elsewhere. The whiskers in Figure~\ref{fig:sobolagreement} are the surrogate's uncertainty about
the index, taken across the \SensRealizations{} posterior realizations. They are not the
confidence intervals SALib \cite{salib} reports, which cover the Monte Carlo error of one Saltelli estimate
on the \SensDesignRows{} point design and run between a fifth and a third of the posterior
width here. Reporting the second in place of the first is how a sensitivity analysis comes to
look far more certain than it is.

\subsection{Along the curve}
\label{sec:functional}
The pointwise indices of Figure~\ref{fig:functional} are the variance based sensitivity analysis
of a parameter indexed process \cite{alexanderian2020}, computed on the \emph{registered}
amplitude functions, and that registration is not a technicality. At a fixed physical displacement near the
peak, the dominant source of variation across runs is whether a given run has reached its peak
yet, so an index computed there on unregistered curves would report which input moves the peak
location rather than which input sets the load. Registration separates the two before the
decomposition, which is why Section~\ref{sec:registration} comes first in this pipeline as well
as in this report. The abscissa is therefore the Karcher mean displacement coordinate: a
physical axis, but a mean one.

The algebra is one matrix product. Each retained score carries its own chaos expansion, and
applying the principal component loadings to those coefficients gives the chaos expansion of
the field at every station, whose variance decomposition follows from orthonormality of the
chaos basis. Because the loadings are orthonormal too, summing the pointwise partial variance
over the stations is exactly the eigenvalue weighted generalized index of Lamboni, Monod and
Makowski \cite{lamboni2011}, so Table~\ref{tab:sensaggregated} and Figure~\ref{fig:functional} are two views of
one decomposition rather than two calculations that happen to sit near each other. A test
asserts that identity rather than trusting the derivation.

\subsection{A prediction that could not be decided}
Build specification 12.3 states the expected physics in one sentence, covers dominating the
service range stiffness and the concrete strength taking over near the peak and through
softening, and before any index existed I committed that sentence to
\texttt{docs/ABLATIONS.md} as five falsifiable predictions, in a commit that precedes the one
adding \texttt{src/ufem/sensitivity.py}. The first and most important of them was that there is
a displacement below which the top cover leads and above which the strength does, with the
crossover before the peak.

There is no crossover. The leading and second ranked inputs swap \SensFuncAmplitudeCrossings{}
times over the \SensFuncAmplitudeStations{} usable stations: \SensFuncAmplitudeLeader{} leads
everywhere, already holding \SensFuncAmplitudeLeadFirst{} of the variance at the first station
the decomposition exists at, \SensFuncAmplitudeFirstStation\,mm, and
\SensFuncAmplitudeRunnerUp{} comes no closer than \SensFuncAmplitudeMinMargin{} behind, at
\SensFuncAmplitudeMinMarginAt\,mm on the softening branch. Read at face value that refutes
prediction one on both its direction and its location. The other two predictions this figure
can decide both hold: the bottom cover's total index never exceeds
\SensFuncAmplitudeMaxTotalCBot{} against a predicted ceiling of 0.10, and the interaction share
never exceeds \SensFuncAmplitudeMaxInteraction{} against a predicted ceiling of 0.15.

But the numbers all three would be read from are the ones Section~\ref{sec:q2gate} withholds,
and a prediction cannot be decided against evidence the same report declines to publish. So the
honest record is that the prediction is undecided on this campaign, with the direction the
withheld numbers ran stated for whoever repeats this on a corrected one. The prediction stays in
\texttt{docs/ABLATIONS.md} unedited, which is the point of having written it down.

The prediction did anticipate one of the two ways this could go wrong, and it is the way it
went. It named the abscissa problem: these indices live on the registered arc length parameter
mapped back through a mean displacement coordinate, so the early part of that axis is not
exactly the early part of any one physical curve. What it did not anticipate is that the whole
question would be undecidable, because the surface being decomposed does not describe the beam
well enough for its decomposition to be about the beam. That is a better reason to have run the
analysis than a confirmation would have been.

\section{Propagation and reliability}
\label{sec:propagation}
The point of everything before this section is to make this one honest. A failure probability is
the easiest number in uncertainty quantification to produce and the hardest to mean: it needs a
sample large enough to resolve it, a surrogate whose error near the threshold is known rather
than assumed, a threshold that is a design quantity rather than a convenient one, and a
statement of where the model was allowed to speak. The predecessor project produced failure
probabilities with none of those, including one against a damage threshold above the value the
material table can reach, whose probability was therefore exactly zero for reasons that had
nothing to do with the beam.

\subsection{Two layers, kept apart}
\label{sec:layers}
Build specification 13.1 requires two uncertainty layers, labeled and never added. The
\emph{aleatory} layer is \MCSamples{} draws from the input distributions of
Section~\ref{sec:design} pushed through the mean surrogate: the spread the beam population
would show if the surrogate were exact. The \emph{epistemic} layer is the surrogate's own
uncertainty, drawn as \MCPosteriorDraws{} samples from the calibrated predictive distribution
at each of \MCEpistemicSubsample{} seeded subsampled input draws. Calibrated is the operative
word: the predictive standard deviation is the fitted one multiplied by the variance scaling
factor Section~\ref{sec:calibration} measured against held out data, so it is a width that was
checked rather than asserted. For the peak load the epistemic standard deviation is
\PropPeakEpistemicShare{} times the aleatory one, which is the honest statement that the
surrogate's uncertainty about a single beam is a substantial fraction of the variation between
beams.

The layers are separated in the code by construction rather than by convention: the epistemic
layer is a function of a mean and a standard deviation, so passing it a zero standard deviation
returns the mean at every draw, exactly, and a test asserts it. That test is the machine
checkable form of binding law 1, since a layer that returned a small nonzero spread from a model
claiming no uncertainty would be manufacturing the thing this project exists not to manufacture.

Sampling is plain Monte Carlo rather than a low discrepancy sequence, deliberately. Build
specification 13.2 asks for a binomial standard error beside every failure probability, and that
standard error is a statement about independent draws; a randomized quasi Monte Carlo estimate
would need a replication based error instead, and two numbers in one table would then mean
different things. One root \texttt{SeedSequence} is spawned into four children, one each for the
input sample, the epistemic subsample, the posterior draws and the curve fan, so no two products
share a stream and none of them depends on the sample size. The Monte Carlo through the Gaussian
processes is matrix algebra: one evaluation of the prior cross covariance between a chunk of
query points and the \NValid{} training points yields the posterior mean, the posterior variance
and all \NValid{} leave one out cross predictions the interval of Section~\ref{sec:bound} needs,
so the whole propagation costs seconds and its memory does not grow with the sample size.

\subsection{Where the model is allowed to speak}
\label{sec:domainmass}
\OutOfDomainPct\,\% of the Monte Carlo mass falls outside the validity domain of
Section~\ref{sec:domain}. That is the largest single qualification on everything in this
section, and it is not a defect of the propagation: it is the censoring of Section~\ref{sec:censoring}
seen from the other end. Roughly half the design failed to converge, the completion model puts
the boundary at $P = \DomainThreshold{}$, and a population sampled from the declared input
distributions therefore lands outside that boundary about half the time. The number appears in
every table and on every figure of this section rather than in a footnote, and the reliability
table also reports each probability restricted to the draws inside the domain, so the size of
the question the censoring leaves open is visible rather than argued about.

\subsection{The peak load threshold, revisited}
\label{sec:characteristic}
Build specification 13.2 asks for the peak load limit state to be set by characteristic value
logic rather than by a multiple of a standard deviation, and the threshold committed with the
configuration skeleton at the start of the project was a placeholder of \PlaceholderPeakKN\,kN
carrying a comment saying so. The characteristic value is the \CharacteristicLevel th percentile
of the propagated capacity distribution, which this stage measures at \CharacteristicPeakKN\,kN.
The placeholder sat \PlaceholderPeakGapPct\,\% below it, which is a different limit state rather
than a rounding, so the configured threshold was revised once to \ConfiguredPeakKN\,kN, the
measured characteristic value rounded down to the nearest 100~N. The revision changed the
configuration hash and every artifact downstream of ingest was regenerated, which is the
mechanism working rather than a cost to be avoided. The stage recomputes the characteristic
value on every run and records its gap to the configured number, \CharacteristicGapPct\,\%, and
a test fails if the two drift apart again.

This makes the peak load limit state partly definitional at the aleatory layer: a threshold set
at the 5th percentile has a failure probability of about 0.05 by construction, and the measured
\PfPeak{} says the arithmetic is consistent. Its informative content is not the point estimate.
It is the gap between that estimate and the surrogate aware bound below, which is what surrogate
error does to a probability at a threshold sitting in the thick part of the distribution.

\subsection{Limit states, with the bound that makes them honest}
\label{sec:bound}
Table~\ref{tab:reliability} is the reliability result. Each row carries a point estimate with
its binomial standard error, a conservative bound, and the out of domain mass fraction, because
none of the three means anything without the other two.

The bound is the answer to a question a point estimate cannot answer: what the surrogate's own
error does to a probability computed from it. A draw counts as a failure whenever its calibrated

\subsection{What is not claimed}
\label{sec:floor}
No failure probability below about $\PfFloor$ is claimed by this report. The limit is not the
Monte Carlo sample size, which resolves \MCSamples{} draws and therefore $10^{-5}$ per draw. It
is the training set: \NValid{} converged runs over three inputs, on a response
Section~\ref{sec:sensdiagnosis} measured to be rough at the design's own resolution, do not
determine the tail of a response surface, and a smooth metamodel extrapolating into that tail
returns a number with no evidence under it. Rarer events need the active learning enrichment of
the follow up campaign, where new finite element runs are placed where the limit state actually
is rather than where the space filling design happened to put them.

The residual capacity row's point estimate sits below that floor, which is exactly the situation
the floor exists to describe rather than to hide.

\subsection{The analytic cross check}
\label{sec:analytic}
Build specification 13.4 requires an independent model. The predecessor project's physics
informed analytical script is reimplemented cleanly for it: same beam, same three input
distributions, same seeded generator, no surrogate anywhere in it. Its geometry survived the
reimplementation and its mechanics did not. The salvaged script evaluated a propped cantilever
deflection formula that belongs to no standard case and to a structural system this model does
not have, under a fixed 50~kN load, divided by an invented strength factor applied on top of a
modulus that is already a function of the strength. What is kept is the idea and the section:
the member of Section~\ref{sec:model} is statically determinate, its bending moment is largest
at the roller with magnitude $P a$ over the 800~mm overhang, the whole member is
in hogging, and the tension reinforcement is therefore the top layer, whose distance from the
compression face at the soffit is exactly the top cover. The capacity is a plane section
rectangular stress block, and the peak load is that capacity divided by the lever arm.

The stated model error is \AnalyticModelErrorPct\,\%, declared before the comparison was run.
A stress block against a nonlinear finite element analysis of the same section is conventionally
good to about ten percent, and four named effects widen it here without cancelling: the block
ignores the concrete tensile contribution, which is not small in a model that defines tension
softening without a fracture energy; the finite element peak occurs at about 11~mm
of imposed displacement, past the plateau of the damaged plasticity compression response; the
mesh is coarse for a bending gradient; and the viscoplastic regularization adds a small rate
dependent overshoot.

The verdict is split, and the split is the finding. The analytic median is
\AnalyticMedianKN\,kN against the surrogate's \SurrogateMedianKN\,kN, a ratio of
\AnalyticMedianRatio{}, so the central tendency \AnalyticCentralVerdict{} within the stated
model error. The dispersion does not: the analytic coefficient of variation is \AnalyticCoV{}
against \SurrogateCoV{}, a factor of \AnalyticDispersionRatio{}, and the analytic 5th percentile
sits \AnalyticLowerRatio{} times the surrogate's, outside the same tolerance the median cleared.
The two distributions in Figure~\ref{fig:analytic} agree about where the capacity is and disagree
about how much it varies.

\subsection{Diagnosing the disagreement}
The elasticities localize it exactly, and this is why they are in Table~\ref{tab:analytic}
rather than only the moments. Regressing the logarithm of the measured peak load on the
logarithms of the three inputs over the \NValid{} runs gives \CampaignElasticityFcm{} for the
strength, \CampaignElasticityCTop{} for the top cover and \CampaignElasticityCBot{} for the
bottom cover, at a fit $R^2$ of \CampaignElasticityRSq{}. The analytic model gives
\AnalyticElasticityFcm{}, \AnalyticElasticityCTop{} and \AnalyticElasticityCBot{}. Two of the
three agree closely. The geometric sensitivity is reproduced almost exactly, which is a strong
statement that the structural idealization is right: the top cover is the effective depth in
both, and both say so with the same number.

The strength elasticity does not agree, and it is not a small disagreement. A section whose
tension steel yields carries its reinforcement force whatever the concrete strength is, so the
strength enters an under reinforced flexural capacity only through the depth of the compression
block, and the analytic elasticity of \AnalyticElasticityFcm{} is what that mechanism implies.
The campaign's \CampaignElasticityFcm{} is more than an order of magnitude larger, and it is
close to the elasticity of the finite element model's own tensile strength scaling,
$((f_{cm} - 8)/20)^{2/3}$, which is \AnalyticTensionElasticity{} at the mean strength, and to
the compression scaling's elasticity of one. The peak load of the inherited campaign is
governed by the concrete constitutive response and not by reinforcement yielding.

That is a statement about the finite element model rather than about either propagation, and it
was predicted by build specification 6.2 before any of this was measured: the campaign defines
tension softening without fracture energy regularization while the strength varies sample to
sample, and it never ran the mesh convergence study its own protocol required. Under that
combination the peak is set by a localization pattern whose strength dependence is the material
table's rather than the section's. The same defect is what Section~\ref{sec:sensdiagnosis}
measured from the other direction as roughness between neighbouring designs. Two phases, two
methods, one cause.

Two further checks are consistent with that reading and are recorded here rather than left as
loose ends. Evaluated at each of the \NValid{} training designs, the analytic capacity correlates
with the measured peak load at \AnalyticPearson{} and overpredicts it by \AnalyticPointwiseBiasPct\,\%
in the mean, which is the correlation a model reproducing only the geometric part of the
variation would show. And the analytic elastic stiffness brackets the measurement without being

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Everything in this section constrains a number that appears earlier in this report. None of it
is a caveat about future work; each item is a reason a reader should discount something already
claimed, and the discount is quantified where the pipeline can quantify it.

\paragraph{The design is censored, and nothing recovers the missing half.}
\NMissing{} of the \NDesign{} designed samples produced no output, the failures cluster at low
top cover and high strength (Section~\ref{sec:censoring}), and the production logs that would
have diagnosed them were not preserved. Every statistic in this report is therefore conditional
on survival. Three things bound the damage rather than hide it: the completion model of
Section~\ref{sec:completion} is calibrated well enough to threshold, the validity domain it
defines is stamped into every artifact and consulted by the dashboard and by the propagation
stage, and the inverse probability weighting study of Section~\ref{sec:weighting} moves the
headline statistics by at most \WeightLargestShift\,\% of the mean, on the
\WeightLargestQoI{}. What none of that fixes is that \OutOfDomainPct\,\% of the Monte Carlo mass
of Section~\ref{sec:propagation} lands outside the domain where the solver ever completed, so
the reliability numbers are extrapolations over a region the campaign never observed. Only new
runs fix this, and rerunning the \NMissing{} is the first spending priority of
Section~\ref{sec:outlook}.

\paragraph{The fracture energy of the inherited campaign is frozen.}
The tension softening table's shape does not move when the strength does
(Section~\ref{sec:model}), so the energy dissipated per unit crack area is an artifact of the
table and the mesh rather than a material property that varies with the sampled concrete. This
is not a stylistic objection. It is the most likely cause of the two hardest findings in this
report, the design roughness of Section~\ref{sec:sensdiagnosis} and the strength elasticity of
Section~\ref{sec:analytic}, and those two are measured by different methods in different
phases. Every response statistic here describes the beam that was simulated; whether it
describes a beam is the question this defect leaves open.

\paragraph{The model parameters that were held fixed are not known to be harmless.}
Dilation angle, viscosity parameter and mesh are constants of the inherited deck, not random
inputs, so every sensitivity statement and every failure probability in this report is
conditional on them. The viscoplastic regularization in particular buys convergence by
dissipating energy, and at a viscosity of $8 \times 10^{-4}$ nobody measured how much. The
route to bounding their influence is one at a time perturbation runs on the mean beam, which
needs the solver and is listed in Section~\ref{sec:outlook}; until those exist, the honest
statement is that the conditioning is unquantified rather than small.

\paragraph{No failure probability below about $\PfFloor$ is resolvable.}
Section~\ref{sec:floor} states the floor and the reason for it: it is set by \NValid{} training
points and plain Monte Carlo through a surrogate, not by the \MCSamples{} sample size, which
would resolve $10^{-5}$ if the surrogate were exact. The reported probabilities sit well above
that floor, and each carries both a binomial standard error and the conservative bound obtained
by counting a failure whenever the calibrated band crosses the threshold. The gap between the
point estimate and that bound, \PfPeak{} against \PfPeakBound{} on the peak load limit state, is
the honest width of the answer at this training set size.

\paragraph{No sensitivity index from this campaign is published, and that is a limitation as
well as a result.}
All \SensWithheld{} of the \SensTargets{} fitted expansions failed the trust gate of
Section~\ref{sec:q2gate}, so this report contains no Sobol index value and no input ranking.
Two independent diagnostics say the campaign rather than the expansion is the reason: the
Gaussian process nuggets cap the explainable variance near the measured $Q^2$ values, and the
model free roughness measurement finds that designs separated by less than
\SensRoughSeparation{} in standardized units differ in peak load by \SensRoughPMax{} times the
whole campaign's standard deviation. A reader who came here for variance decompositions leaves
without them. What replaces them is the measurement that says why, which is worth more than an
index nobody should trust, but it is not what was asked for.

\paragraph{The peak load of this campaign is governed by the material card, not by the
section.}
The elasticity diagnosis of Section~\ref{sec:analytic} is the sharpest single limitation in
this report. The campaign's logarithmic elasticity of peak load with respect to strength is
\CampaignElasticityFcm{}, against \AnalyticElasticityFcm{} from an independent mechanics model
of the same beam, while the two agree on the geometric elasticities. An under reinforced
section whose steel yields cannot have that strength sensitivity; a localization pattern
following the finite element model's own tensile scaling can, and does. So the surrogate,
the calibration and the reliability analysis are all faithful to a finite element model whose
peak load mechanism is not the one the structure would exhibit. The surrogate is not wrong
about the campaign. The campaign is wrong about the beam, in a way that is now measured rather
than suspected.

\paragraph{The ablations bound the design choices, they do not settle them.}
Section~\ref{sec:ablations} reports where the shipped representation wins and where it does
not, and the honest summary is mixed: registration earns its place on component count and peak
bias, and the reconstruction path through warps and a displacement coordinate costs enough at
curve level that simpler direct curve models match or beat it. Every ablation is a single seeded
run of one architecture at one sample size, with no hyperparameter search on either side, which
is the only fair comparison available at \NValid{} runs and is also a thin one. A different
architecture, or the same one with ten times the data, could reverse any of these rows.

\section{Outlook: the corrected campaign}
\label{sec:outlook}
Everything in Section~\ref{sec:limitations} that is not about sample size is about the finite
element model, and all of it needs solver time rather than better statistics. The corrected
campaign is specified in build specification section 14 and is ordered by what it would fix
here, not by what is easiest.

\textbf{Fracture energy coupling first.} The material generator becomes a deterministic function
of the sampled strength for the whole concrete damaged plasticity card rather than for the
compression branch alone: $f_{ctm}$ from the strength, a fracture energy from the fib Model Code
2010 relation, and tension softening declared by displacement or by fracture energy so the
softening branch is regularized against the element size. That is the direct correction to the
defect Section~\ref{sec:model} names and Sections~\ref{sec:sensdiagnosis}
and~\ref{sec:analytic} measure. If it works, the roughness that withheld every sensitivity index
should fall, and the strength elasticity should move toward the mechanism the analytic model
describes. Both are testable predictions rather than hopes, and they should be written down
before the first corrected run, exactly as the ablation predictions of
Section~\ref{sec:ablations} were.

\textbf{Then a mesh convergence study, including a deliberate counterexample.} Three meshes on
the mean beam with the corrected card, requiring the peak within 3 percent and the absorbed
energy within 5 percent between the two finest, plus one pair run with an unregularized strain
based softening definition to exhibit the non convergence being avoided. That pair is the single
most useful figure the follow up campaign can produce, because it shows the reader the failure
mode this report inherited rather than describing it.

\textbf{Then a benchmark the corrected card has to reproduce before it is believed.} Build
specification 14.5 gates the corrected material generator on one external reproduction, the
modern re execution of the Bresler Scordelis beams with measured post peak response
\cite{vecchio2004}, run with the same generator and scored against the published measurements
on peak load, deflection at peak, absorbed energy and failure mode. The failure mode is part of
the gate and not a remark on it: a card that reaches the right load through the wrong mechanism
is exactly the defect Section~\ref{sec:analytic} measured in the inherited campaign, and a
tolerance on the load alone would pass it again.

\textbf{Then the \NMissing{} missing runs.} Rerunning the censored corner under the corrected
setup attacks the bias directly, and it is the only thing that does. The completion model is
refit after every batch and the validity domain regenerated, so the effect on the domain is
measured rather than assumed.

\textbf{Then the fourth input.} Promoting the strength to stiffness model error to an
independent lognormal multiplier on the Eurocode~2 expression dissolves the collinearity of
Section~\ref{sec:design} by reparameterization instead of by the Shapley machinery that
dependent inputs would otherwise force \cite{iooss2019}, and it makes the
scatter that the inherited campaign suppressed into a measurable sensitivity. It costs one extra
sampled dimension, which at three inputs is affordable and at six would not be.

\textbf{Then active learning where the reliability answer is thin.} With a corrected and
uncensored design in place, 30 to 50 enrichment points chosen by a $U$ learning function
\cite{echard2011} over a
kriging surrogate, in batches, targeting the peak load limit state, is what moves the resolvable
failure probability below the $\PfFloor$ floor of Section~\ref{sec:floor}. The learning function
and the stopping rule are taken from the active learning reliability benchmark
\cite{moustapha2022} rather than chosen here, because a method picked for a single limit state
is a method fitted to it. Doing it before the
physics is corrected would spend solver time refining the answer to the wrong question.

Two things do not need the solver and are worth stating as the smaller half of the outlook. The
curve level result of Section~\ref{sec:baselines}, where three simple baselines beat the
reconstruction pipeline, points at the reconstruction rather than at the reduction, and a direct
comparison of the two paths on the same scores would localize it. And the functional conformal
bands of Section~\ref{sec:functionalbands}, which are this project's one methodological
increment, are validated here on a single family of softening curves; a second family would say
whether the modulation choice generalizes.

\section{Conclusion}
The campaign delivered \NValid{} fully converged, softening resolved load displacement and damage histories over a three dimensional input space, with clean, information rich response statistics. It also delivered two hard lessons that the successor pipeline is built around. Half the design was lost to a biased, undiagnosed failure mode, and the response to that is neither to ignore it nor to discard the data, but to model it: a calibrated completion probability model, a validity domain derived from it that every downstream stage must consult, and a weighting study that bounds the residual bias at a few percent of the mean. And two of the nominal outputs, the fourth input and the terminal damage, carry no independent information at all, which the predecessor project propagated anyway.

On that campaign the pipeline delivers what it claimed it would and refuses what it cannot
support, and the second half of that sentence is the part worth keeping. The surrogate beats
every mandatory baseline out of sample on the four headline scalar quantities and loses to three
of them on the whole reconstructed curve, and Section~\ref{sec:ablations} then finds four
further models that do the same, which localizes the loss to the reconstruction path rather than
to the reduction. The calibration gate passed: simultaneous \MCBandLevel{} percent functional
bands and scalar jackknife+ intervals both reach \BandForceCoverage{} empirical coverage out of
sample, with the correction factor measured rather than styled. The sensitivity gate did not
pass, so this report publishes no Sobol index and publishes instead the two diagnostics that say
why, one of which, the roughness between neighbouring designs, points straight back at the
unregularized softening definition named in Section~\ref{sec:model}. The reliability numbers
carry a standard error, a surrogate aware bound and the fraction of their own sample that sits
outside the domain where the solver ever completed.

The single most consequential finding is not any of those. It is that the peak load of this
campaign moves with the concrete strength an order of magnitude more strongly than the section's
mechanics allow (Section~\ref{sec:analytic}), which means the model that produced the data is
answering a different question from the beam. Correcting that is the first item of
Section~\ref{sec:outlook} and it needs the solver, not more statistics.
